The Story

Old rivalries. Buried secrets. A small town that remembers everything.

When Trina Leah uncovers a hidden decree tucked inside her mother's cedar chest, the still waters of her life begin to stir. Old rivalries surface. Long-buried secrets awaken. And the meaning of a mysterious headstone carved with the words "Teton Angel" threatens to reveal things never meant to be discovered.

Moving between the ranches of eastern Idaho and the streets of Jackson Hole, and across fifteen years, Teton Angel follows the Robinson family and the people they love: a rancher patriarch with a quiet past, his four very different sons, a young rodeo rider who never forgot a kindness, and a woman whose survival depends on men she never saw coming.

Jean Polson was working at the gas station in St. Anthony, Idaho. Kenny Polson had entered less than five minutes earlier.

"Good morning, Jean, how is your day going?"

Jean and Kenny were not on good terms, shall we say. They had always been battling companions on different sides of the line. Jean married Kenny's older brother when he was just out of high school, and the childish antics of both continued into his older brother's marriage.

No one knew where this hostility ever started. Both were strong-willed and never gave an inch. Kenny had a way of pushing Jean's buttons, and there was no need to add to the flavors of combat.

When Kenny's brother died unexpectedly in an unfortunate accident, Jean and Kenny had not had much to say to each other. The lines that they fought over disappeared, and Kenny just quit talking to Jean, his brother's wife, that very same day.

"That will be $6.25," Jean said as she greeted Kenny at the register. "How much gas are you getting today, Kenny?"

"Put $15 on pump number three."

Then, unexpectedly, Jean hesitated. "Kenny, I would like to ask you for some help. A favor."

Kenny stopped mid-turn. That was not something he expected.

The Author

Roland Hunter

Roland Hunter was raised on a purebred livestock ranch. His father was widely admired in cattle circles: 100 live purebred calves from 100 cows, a ranch photographed for a livestock magazine. Roland went on to work four different purebred livestock farms, and when an economic downturn sent him looking for work in other regions, he never stopped longing for the western life he came from.

Around 2000 he began writing a novel, one paragraph and one line at a time. Years later, an automobile accident that wasn't his fault cost him his commercial driver's license. During the recovery he went back to the manuscript and finished it. What began as a setback became the chance to complete a lifelong dream.

Everyone has a story within them. This is his.
