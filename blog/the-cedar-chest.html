The Cedar Chest: The Secrets We Keep Out of Love

November 6, 2026 · 4 min read

← All posts

Nearly every family has one. A cedar chest at the foot of the bed, a shoebox on a closet shelf, a drawer nobody else opens. Inside are the things someone couldn't throw away and couldn't quite explain.

In Teton Angel, it sits at the foot of Kasey's bed, and it's where the mystery begins. When Roland Hunter got the book's trailer back, seeing that chest come to life on screen was one of the moments that warmed his heart. So here is the story behind it. This post stays inside the first few chapters, so there's nothing here to spoil the book for you.

A chest full of good memories

For most of her life, the chest is one of Trina Leah's favorite places. She and her mother sit together and unfold the blankets, the handmade crafts and the delicate linens, trading stories over old photographs of family she never got to meet. Her favorite picture shows her mother with a chipmunk perched on her shoulder.

That's what makes the chest such a good place to start a mystery. There's nothing frightening about it. It's the place where Trina Leah has always felt most connected to the people she comes from.

The paper at the bottom

At fifteen, she looks deeper than she ever has. She pulls out her parents' wedding album and, for the first time, actually reads the names and dates. Then an envelope falls out of her mother's veterinary certificate. On its worn front are the words Dear Family. Beneath it is a paper she has never seen: Final Decree of Adoption.

It's dated the day her parents were married.

She goes straight for her mother's diaries, and they only make it worse. The entries stop in June 2003, right after a college graduation, and don't pick up again until her mother is engaged. Two years of silence, from a woman who wrote everything down. Trina Leah checks the dates again, and they don't add up.

Everyone who loves her is keeping quiet

She can't ask her mother, at least not again. She already has, and Kasey told her she isn't ready. So Trina Leah walks up to the hillside cemetery with Miss Leah, the woman who chose to become her grandmother, and asks her instead. She asks about the decree. She asks about the missing years. She asks about the stone her mother always stops beside, the one that says Roy Polson, Son.

Miss Leah knows the answers. She also made Kasey a promise long ago, and she tells her granddaughter so.

“I cannot break it, not even for you.”

Then the book does something quietly kind. It takes you to Kasey, curled up beside her husband Aiden, asking him how she is ever going to tell their daughter. She is afraid of what the truth will do to the girl she loves more than anything. Aiden tells her she can share it a little at a time, and that if she tells it with love, Trina Leah will be okay.

Nobody in this house is cruel. Nobody is hiding anything to get ahead. Each of them is holding on to something because they love someone.

What love keeps, and what it costs

Roland describes Teton Angel as a story about faith, family and the secrets we keep as a family out of love. That last part is harder than it sounds. A parent can keep a secret to protect a child, and the child can still feel the shape of it. Trina Leah is right that she's old enough to ask. Kasey is right to be afraid. Miss Leah is right to keep her word. The book lets all three of those be true at once.

And hers isn't the only family in the valley living with what was never said. If you want to know who's who before you start, our spoiler-light guide to the Robinsons will get you oriented. If you'd like to see how the chest and the headstone ended up on the cover, that's in He Painted It First.

A few questions for your table

If your book club picks up Teton Angel, or if you just have a chest of your own at home, these are good places to start:

  • When is a child old enough to hear a hard family truth, and who gets to decide?
  • Is a promise to keep someone's secret ever worth breaking for someone else you love?
  • Who holds the stories in your family? Have you asked them yet?

That last one may be the one to act on. The chest will keep. The person who can explain what's in it might not be around forever.
